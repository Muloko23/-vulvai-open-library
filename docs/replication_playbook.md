# Replication Playbook

How any community organization can bring Know My Body to girls in its own region.

Rajasthan is the first deployment. Every part of this project is designed so that a new community can launch its own culturally competent version without starting from zero.

## What every new site starts with

* The open lesson library, with eight modules covering the core of body literacy
* The learning path engine, library retrieval, and safety routing
* The offline Know My Body app
* The outcomes toolkit, so every site measures the same six outcomes
* The facilitator guide and the cocreation and clinical review workflow

## Six steps to launch in a new community

| Step | What happens | Who leads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Partner | A trusted local organization that already works with girls and families joins as the community partner | Local partner and VULVAi |
| 2. Listen | A youth advisory group of local girls and mothers names the questions, beliefs, and barriers that matter in their community | Local partner |
| 3. Adapt | The group reshapes stories, names, images, and examples, and translators produce the local languages | Local partner and translators |
| 4. Verify | Clinicians check every adapted lesson for medical accuracy and add local referral information | VULVAi clinicians with local health providers |
| 5. Launch | Facilitators are trained, the library is loaded onto shared phones, and sessions begin | Local partner |
| 6. Measure and share | The site measures the six outcomes and contributes its adapted lessons back to the open library | Local partner and VULVAi |

## Why it scales

* **The medical core is reused.** Anatomy and physiology are the same everywhere, so each new site adapts how lessons are told rather than rebuilding what they say.
* **The library grows with every site.** Each community adds its languages, stories, and lessons, making the next adaptation faster.
* **The evidence grows with every site.** Every site uses the same outcomes toolkit, building a shared evidence base on what changes when girls learn about their bodies.
* **It runs on what communities already have.** Shared phones, offline use, and existing community networks keep the cost per girl low.

## Path after Rajasthan

1. A second Indian state, through B.Bindi's and Bindi International's networks
2. Partner sites in other regions, identified through VULVAi's networks, including the Echoing Green global fellowship
3. Adoption by ministries of health and education and by international organizations, using open content that can enter public programs without license fees
